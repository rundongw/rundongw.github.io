## Research interests

I keep coming back to one question in different guises: how does a mind that
lives inside a single point of view manage to deal with a world that is
constantly moving, full of other agents, and only ever partially in view?

- **Time and dynamics** — how the visual system summarizes and segments a
  continuously changing scene: ensemble perception, event boundaries, and how
  we come to experience time as a lived, continuous thing rather than a series
  of snapshots.
- **Social cognition** — how observers read and anticipate the actions of
  other people, and how that ability shifts across situations and across
  individuals.
- **Neurophenomenology** — what it means for experience to always be *from* a
  point of view, and how phenomenology (Husserl, Merleau-Ponty) can inform
  questions that cognitive science usually asks in purely computational terms.
